Animal health knowledge base

🐄 Dairy & beef cattle

Track appetite, milk yield, temperature if safely measured, coughing, diarrhoea, lameness, calving and udder changes. Conditions needing professional assessment include mastitis, tick-borne diseases, bloat, pneumonia, lumpy skin disease and foot-and-mouth disease.

🐐 Goats & sheep

Record sudden deaths, mouth lesions, nasal discharge, diarrhoea, coughing, abortions and swollen joints. PPR, CCPP, parasites and nutritional problems can resemble one another; testing and examination matter.

🐔 Poultry

Track daily deaths, feed and water intake, egg production, droppings and respiratory or neurological signs. Suspected avian influenza or Newcastle disease requires prompt veterinary advice and movement controls.

🐖 Pigs

Watch fever, skin discoloration, weakness, abortions and sudden deaths. Suspected ASF: stop animal movements, restrict visitors and equipment, avoid untreated swill, and contact veterinary authorities immediately.

🐕 Dogs & cats

Bite exposure, suspected rabies, seizures, poisoning, inability to urinate, severe bleeding or breathing trouble are emergencies. Avoid handling animals with unusual aggression or neurological signs; seek professional help.

🌾 Prevention & farm records

Maintain vaccination and deworming records with veterinary advice, quarantine new arrivals, control ticks and rodents, clean equipment, protect feed and water, and log illness, treatment and withdrawal periods.

Symptom report assistant

Enter observations to create a structured report you can show a veterinary professional. It cannot identify a disease with certainty.

When to escalate urgently

  • Several animals sick at once or unusual/sudden deaths.
  • Suspected ASF in pigs, foot-and-mouth disease, PPR, anthrax, rabies or avian influenza.
  • Severe breathing difficulty, collapse, inability to stand, seizures or major bleeding.
  • Abortion clusters, unusual blisters/mouth lesions, or sudden drop in poultry egg production with deaths.

What to do: minimise contact and movement, separate sick animals if safe, keep children away, use dedicated footwear/equipment, and contact a licensed veterinarian or county veterinary office. Do not administer human medicines or leftover antibiotics.

Veterinary research & quality governance

MKULIMA is designed around a multidisciplinary review model covering clinical veterinary medicine, epidemiology, pathology, microbiology, parasitology, poultry and pig health, dairy production, animal nutrition, public health, data science, and county field practice. This is a proposed team structure—not a claim that 15 veterinarians have been hired or have reviewed this page.

Before clinical deployment, disease content should be reviewed by registered Kenyan veterinary professionals, date-stamped, sourced, version-controlled and checked against Directorate of Veterinary Services guidance. AI outputs must communicate uncertainty, protect farmer data, and never replace laboratory confirmation or official outbreak declarations.
